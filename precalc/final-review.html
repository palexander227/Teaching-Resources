Precalculus Final Examination Study Guide

Use this guide to review the kinds of ideas, techniques, and traps that matter most. The examples below are similar in spirit to the final, but use different functions and numbers.

Overview Part I Part II Part III Essay Strategy

How to Use This Guide

The strongest preparation strategy is not memorization alone. You should practice understanding definitions precisely, recognizing hidden domain restrictions, identifying when a theorem does or does not apply, and developing fluency with algebraic structure.

Part I: numeric response, algebraic fluency, exact integer answers.
Part II: multiple choice, structural recognition, interpretation.
Part III: select all that apply, conceptual precision and counterexamples.
Essay: theorem statements, explanation, and mathematical communication.

Part I — Numeric Response

1. Rational Functions and Removable Discontinuities

You should be able to factor polynomials, simplify rational expressions, identify holes, and determine excluded domain values.

Practice-Type Example

If \[ f(x)=\frac{x^2-16}{x-4}, \] then factor first: \[x^2-16=(x-4)(x+4).\] Thus \[f(x)=x+4\] for all \(x\neq4\). The graph behaves like a line with a hole.

  • Can I identify the excluded value from the original denominator?
  • Can I distinguish a hole from a vertical asymptote?
  • Can I simplify only after stating the domain restriction?

2. Continuity of Piecewise Functions

A piecewise function is continuous at a transition point only when the left-hand value, right-hand value, and function value agree.

Practice-Type Example

Suppose \[ f(x)=\begin{cases}5x-2,&x<1\\ ax+6,&x\ge 1.\end{cases} \] To make \(f\) continuous at \(x=1\), set the two pieces equal at the transition point.

3. Tangent Function and Interval Structure

Know where tangent is undefined and how transformations change those locations.

For \(f(x)=\tan(2x)\), solve \[2x=\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi.\] Then count the resulting discontinuities or intervals carefully.

4. Polynomial Roots and Vieta’s Formulas

For a cubic \(ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\), the sum of roots is \(-b/a\).

For \[x^3+4x^2-7x+9,\] if the roots are \(r_1,r_2,r_3\), then \[r_1+r_2+r_3=-4.\]

5. Quadratic and Quartic Optimization

Quartics in \(x^2\) can often be treated like quadratics by substitution.

For \[x^4-6x^2+8,\] let \(u=x^2\). Then analyze \[u^2-6u+8.\]

6. Profit Functions and Quadratics

For \(ax^2+bx+c\), the vertex occurs at \[x=-\frac{b}{2a}.\]

Analyze \[P(x)=-3x^2+72x-180.\] Find the maximizing production level, break-even points, and useful root relationships.

7. Exponents and Logarithms

This is one of the heaviest themes on the final. Practice exponent laws, log laws, change of base, and domain restrictions.

Simplify: \[(32x^4y^{-2})^{3/5}.\]

Expand: \[\log_3(27x^2y^{-5}).\]

Rewrite: \[\ln(e^5x^{-2}y^4).\]

Warning: The power rule \(\log_b(x^r)=r\log_b(x)\) requires appropriate positivity conditions in the real-number system.

8. Exponential Equations

Rewrite both sides using common bases whenever possible.

Solve \[27^{x+1}=9^{2x-1}.\] Rewrite everything using powers of \(3\).

9. Rational Inequalities and Sign Charts

Factor the numerator and denominator, locate zeros and undefined points, then test intervals.

Analyze \[\frac{x^2-9}{x^2-x-6}>0.\]

Critical distinction: zeros of the denominator are never included.

10. Average Rate of Change and Secant Lines

Average rate of change is \[\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}.\]

For \[f(x)=4x^2-7x+2,\] compute the average rate of change on \([a,a+2]\). Also practice writing secant-line equations.

11. Radical Equations

Isolate radicals first, square carefully, and check for extraneous solutions.

Solve \[\sqrt{x+7}=11-x.\]

Part II — Multiple Choice

Part II emphasizes structural reasoning, recognition, equivalent forms, and interpretation.

Coordinate Geometry

If slope is \(m\), perpendicular slope is \(-1/m\). Practice identifying points on perpendicular lines through a given point.

Algebraic Structure

Recognize perfect squares, differences of squares, and hidden substitutions such as \((x-k)^2=x^2-2kx+k^2\).

Exponential Models

Interpret expressions such as \[A(t)=12000(1.04)^{12t}.\] What does the exponent scaling mean?

Polynomial Factor Theorem

If \(x-a\) is a factor, then \(f(a)=0\). Use this to test possible factors quickly.

Systems and Tangency

When a line intersects a parabola at exactly one point, the discriminant is zero. For \(ax^2+bx+c\), \[\Delta=b^2-4ac.\]

Part III — Conceptual Questions

This section rewards precision. Memorized slogans are dangerous. Always ask: is this statement always true, sometimes true, or false?

Complex Numbers

For \(z=a+bi\), \(\overline z=a-bi\), and \(|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\).

Practice with \(z=2-3i\): compute its modulus, conjugate, reciprocal, and powers.

Roots of Unity

Know the geometric picture: roots of unity lie on a circle and often form regular polygons.

Analyze \[z^4=16.\] Identify modulus, real roots, imaginary roots, sum, product, and geometry.

Logarithms

Know domains, continuity, asymptotic behavior, growth behavior, and legal vs illegal simplifications.

Common trap: \[\log(x+y)\neq\log x+\log y.\]

Analyze \[f(x)=\log_5(x^2-1).\] Find domain, continuity intervals, asymptotic behavior, and zeros.

Continuity and IVT

The Intermediate Value Theorem requires continuity on \([a,b]\).

The converse is false: a function can have the intermediate value property without being continuous.

Polynomial Theory

Review the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, conjugate roots, multiplicity, and odd/even degree behavior.

If a real-coefficient polynomial has root \(4-7i\), what other root must it have?

Rational Functions and Asymptotes

Know how to identify holes, vertical asymptotes, horizontal asymptotes, and slant asymptotes.

Analyze \[f(x)=\frac{x^3-8}{x^2-4}.\] Determine discontinuities, asymptotes, and whether the graph can intersect an asymptote.

A graph may intersect a horizontal or slant asymptote.

Sequences and Series

Know boundedness, monotonicity, oscillation, convergence, divergence tests, geometric series, arithmetic series, harmonic series, and telescoping series.

Determine convergence of \[\left(1-\frac1n\right)^n\] and \[\frac{\cos n}{n}.\]

Divergence Test: If \(\sum a_n\) converges, then \(a_n\to0\). But \(a_n\to0\) does not guarantee that \(\sum a_n\) converges.
Geometric Series: \(\sum ar^n\) converges iff \(|r|<1\).
Oscillation: oscillating does not necessarily mean bounded. For example, \((-1)^nn\) oscillates and is unbounded.

Essay Questions

The essay questions test mathematical communication, theorem precision, conceptual explanation, and logical structure. Practice writing complete sentences.

Essay Topic 1 — Fundamental Theorem of Algebra

You should be able to state:

Every nonconstant polynomial with complex coefficients has at least one complex root. Equivalently, every degree \(n\) polynomial over \(\mathbb C\) factors completely into linear factors over \(\mathbb C\).

Irreducible Quadratics

Over the real numbers, irreducible means the quadratic cannot factor into real linear factors. Equivalently, its discriminant is negative.

Practice factoring \[x^4+16\] into irreducible real quadratics.

Essay Topic 2 — Logarithm Laws and Domain Issues

Understand why a quotient inside one logarithm may exist even when the two separate logarithms do not.

Explain why \[\log\left(\frac{x+3}{x-5}\right)\] may exist even when \[\log(x+3)-\log(x-5)\] does not.

The quotient rule \[\log\left(\frac ab\right)=\log a-\log b\] requires \(a>0\) and \(b>0\) individually. But \(a/b>0\) can occur when both \(a\) and \(b\) are negative.

Final Preparation Strategy

Most Important Skills

  1. Factor aggressively.
  2. Watch domains constantly.
  3. Distinguish equations from identities.
  4. Know theorem hypotheses.
  5. Check whether statements are always true or only sometimes true.
  6. Use counterexamples.
  7. Avoid memorized slogans without conditions.

Highest Priority Topics

  • Logarithms and exponentials
  • Rational functions
  • Polynomial theory
  • Continuity and IVT
  • Sequences and series
  • Roots of unity
  • Asymptotic behavior
Best study method: redo old quests and reviews, build your own counterexamples, explain concepts aloud, and practice recognizing when a theorem does not apply.
