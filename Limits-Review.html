Unit Review: Limits

Problem 1. Algebraic Limits

Evaluate each of the following limits, if they exist.

$$\text{(i)}\quad \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2 - 1}{x + 1}$$ $$\text{(ii)}\quad \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^3 - 8}{x - 2}$$ $$\text{(iii)}\quad \lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^3 - 8}{x - 2}$$ $$\text{(iv)}\quad \lim_{x \to y} \frac{x^n - y^n}{x - y}$$ $$\text{(v)}\quad \lim_{y \to x} \frac{x^n - y^n}{x - y}$$ $$\text{(vi)}\quad \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{a+h} - \sqrt{a}}{h}$$

Problem 2. Limits and the Difference Quotient

Let $f(x) = x^{2/3}$. Recall that the derivative of $f$ at $a$ may be defined by

$$f'(a) = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a}.$$

(a) Find $f'(8)$.
(b) Find $f'(a)$, where $a$ is any nonzero real number.

Problem 3. Radical Limits

Find the following limits, if they exist.

$$\text{(i)}\quad \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{1-\sqrt{x}}{1-x}$$ $$\text{(ii)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1-\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x}$$ $$\text{(iii)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1-\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x^2}$$

Problem 4. Trigonometric Limits

$$\text{(i)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(2x)}{x}$$ $$\text{(ii)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(ax)}{\sin(bx)}$$ $$\text{(iii)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin^2(2x)}{x}$$ $$\text{(iv)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin^2(2x)}{x^2}$$ $$\text{(v)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1-\cos x}{x^2}$$ $$\text{(vi)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan^2 x + 2x}{x + x^2}$$ $$\text{(vii)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x\sin x}{1-\cos x}$$ $$\text{(viii)}\quad \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\sin(x+h)-\sin x}{h}$$ $$\text{(ix)}\quad \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\sin(x^2-1)}{x-1}$$ $$\text{(x)}\quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x^2(3+\sin x)}{(x+\sin x)^2}$$ $$\text{(xi)}\quad \lim_{x \to 1} (x^2-1)^3 \sin\!\left(\frac{1}{x-1}\right)$$

Problem 5. An Exponential Limit

Use the approximation $e^h \approx 1+h$ for small $h$, together with suitable algebra, to evaluate

$$\lim_{x \to a} \frac{e^x - e^a}{x-a}.$$

Problem 6. Justifying the Approximation

Using the limit definition of the exponential function, verify that

$$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{e^h - 1}{h} = 1,$$

and explain how this justifies the approximation used in Problem 5.

Problem 7. A Geometric Limit Argument

Using a geometric argument involving slices of the unit circle, explain heuristically why

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1.$$

Problem 8. Trigonometric Limits and Derivatives

(a) Argue why $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1-\cos x}{x} = 0.$$

(b) Using a limit definition of the derivative and results established earlier, prove that $$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x.$$

(c) Derive small-angle approximations for $\sin x$ and $\cos x$.

Challenge Problem

Using the small-angle approximations for $\sin x$ and $\cos x$, argue that

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x\cos x - \sin x}{x^2} = 0.$$
