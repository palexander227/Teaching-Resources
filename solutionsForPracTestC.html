Problem 1 — Surveying and the SSA Ambiguous Case

Let triangle \(ABT\) have \(AB=300\) meters, \(BT=210\) meters, and \(\angle A=35^\circ\). Since \(BT\) is opposite \(\angle A\), the Law of Sines gives

\[ \frac{\sin T}{300}=\frac{\sin 35^\circ}{210}. \]

Therefore

\[ \sin T = \frac{300\sin 35^\circ}{210}\approx 0.8194. \]

This gives two possible angles:

\[ T\approx 55.0^\circ \qquad\text{or}\qquad T\approx 125.0^\circ. \]
Case\(\angle T\)\(\angle B\)\(AT\)
1\(55.0^\circ\)\(90.0^\circ\)\(366.1\) m
2\(125.0^\circ\)\(20.0^\circ\)\(125.4\) m

The two possible triangles are produced by the ambiguous SSA situation. If the geographic information tells us that the tower must be on the north side of \(AB\), then the valid diagram is the one drawn with \(T\) north of \(AB\).

Teacher note: The “north of \(AB\)” condition eliminates a reflected south-side drawing. If both SSA triangles are drawn on the north side of \(AB\), then that condition alone does not distinguish between the two SSA solutions. To force exactly one numerical solution, add one more requirement such as “the tower is east of \(B\)” or “the tower is less than 200 meters from \(A\).”

Problem 2 — Rational Function Analysis

Analyze

\[ f(x)=\frac{x^3+3x^2-4}{x^3-3x^2-x+3}. \]

1. Factor completely

\[ x^3+3x^2-4=(x-1)(x+2)^2 \] \[ x^3-3x^2-x+3=(x-1)(x-3)(x+1) \]

So

\[ f(x)=\frac{(x-1)(x+2)^2}{(x-1)(x-3)(x+1)}. \]

For \(x\ne 1\), this simplifies to

\[ f(x)=\frac{(x+2)^2}{(x-3)(x+1)}. \]

2. Intercepts, asymptotes, hole, domain

  • Hole: \(x=1\). The simplified value is \(\frac{(1+2)^2}{(1-3)(1+1)}=-\frac94\), so the hole is \(\left(1,-\frac94\right)\).
  • Vertical asymptotes: \(x=-1\) and \(x=3\).
  • Horizontal asymptote: \(y=1\).
  • \(x\)-intercept: \((-2,0)\). Since \((x+2)^2\) has even multiplicity, the graph touches the axis there.
  • \(y\)-intercept: \(f(0)=-\frac43\), so \(\left(0,-\frac43\right)\).
  • Domain: \(x\ne -1,1,3\).

3. Relative maximum and minimum

Using the simplified function,

\[ f'(x)=\frac{-2(x+2)(3x+1)}{(x-3)^2(x+1)^2}. \]

The critical points occur at \(x=-2\) and \(x=-\frac13\).

  • At \(x=-2\), \(f(-2)=0\). This is a relative maximum.
  • At \(x=-\frac13\), \(f\left(-\frac13\right)=-\frac54\). This is a relative minimum.

4. Range

Solving \(y=\frac{(x+2)^2}{(x-3)(x+1)}\) for possible values of \(y\) gives the range

\[ (-\infty,-\tfrac54]\cup[0,\infty). \]

Problem 3 — Sinusoidal Buoy Model

The buoy has maximum height \(18\) ft at \(t=1\), and minimum height \(6\) ft at \(t=4\).

\[ \text{Amplitude}=\frac{18-6}{2}=6 \] \[ \text{Midline}=\frac{18+6}{2}=12 \]

Going from maximum to minimum takes \(4-1=3\) hours, which is half a period. Therefore the full period is

\[ P=6. \]

Thus

\[ B=\frac{2\pi}{P}=\frac{2\pi}{6}=\frac{\pi}{3}. \]

One suitable model

Since the buoy is at a maximum when \(t=1\), cosine is the natural choice:

\[ h(t)=6\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3}(t-1)\right)+12. \]

This model starts at the midline before \(t=1\), reaches a maximum at \((1,18)\), reaches a minimum at \((4,6)\), and completes a full cycle by \((7,18)\).
