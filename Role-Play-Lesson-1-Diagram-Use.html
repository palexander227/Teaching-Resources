How to Score in the 90s on the DECA Principles of Finance Role Play

What You Are Actually Being Scored On

Student Takeaway:
Judges are scoring whether you can explain why prices increased using clear economic cause-and-effect.

Diagram 1: Supply Shift (CORE DIAGRAM)

Student Task: You should be able to hand-draw this diagram in under 30 seconds.
What to Say While Pointing:
“Demand stayed about the same, but higher fuel and labor costs made supply more expensive. When supply shifts left, equilibrium prices rise.”

Diagram 2: Prices as Signals

Student Takeaway:
Prices are not just numbers — they send signals.
What to Say:
“Higher prices signal scarcity and higher costs. They tell producers and consumers how to respond.”

Diagram 3: Economic Cause-and-Effect Chain

Student Takeaway:
This diagram explains why spending rose even though income and habits stayed the same.
What to Say:
“The increase moved through the system — nothing changed about your income or consumption.”

Diagram 4: Stable Demand vs Changing Supply

What to Say:
“You’re buying the same amount — market conditions changed around you.”

Diagram 5: Consumer Substitution Strategy

What to Say:
“When prices rise, consumers substitute lower-cost goods to reduce spending without reducing consumption.”

Final Rule

If you draw a diagram, you must explain it.
If you explain it clearly, judges can justify giving you 15–18s.
