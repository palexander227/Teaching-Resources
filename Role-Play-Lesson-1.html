How to Score in the 90s on the DECA Principles of Finance Role Play

What You Are Actually Being Scored On

Student Takeaway:
Judges are not scoring how many terms you know. They are scoring whether you can explain why prices changed in a way that makes sense to a real client.
Student Takeaway:
High scores come from explaining cause and effect: what changed in the economy → how that affected prices → why the client’s budget broke.

Performance Indicator 1: Economics & Economic Activity

Student Takeaway:
When you define economics, you must immediately connect it to the client’s problem.
Low-Scoring Answer:
“Economics is the study of how people make choices with limited resources.”
90+ Answer:
“Economics explains how limited resources like labor, fuel, and food are allocated. When those resources become more expensive, prices rise—even if consumers buy the same amount.”
Student Rule:
Always define economics, then apply it directly to the client’s higher spending.

Performance Indicator 2: Factors Affecting the Business Environment

Student Takeaway:
You must name specific factors that caused prices to rise. General statements score lower.
  • Higher labor costs
  • Higher fuel and transportation costs
  • Supply chain disruptions
  • Government policy (interest rates, regulation)
90+ Answer:
“Grocery prices increased because producers faced higher fuel and labor costs. Those higher costs were passed through the supply chain to consumers.”
Student Rule:
If you explain how businesses are affected, judges hear professionalism.

Performance Indicator 3: Function of Prices in Markets

Student Takeaway:
This is where many students lose points. Saying “prices balance supply and demand” is not enough.
Average Answer:
“Prices help balance supply and demand.”
90+ Answer:
“Prices act as signals. Higher prices signal scarcity and rising costs, not increased consumption. That’s why your expenses rose even though your buying habits didn’t change.”
Student Rule:
Always explain what prices are signaling.

Performance Indicator 4: Supply and Demand

Student Takeaway:
You must explain direction and cause, not just name supply and demand.
90+ Answer:
“Demand for groceries stayed about the same, but supply became more expensive. When supply shifts left and demand stays constant, prices rise.”
Student Rule:
Say what moved, why it moved, and what happened to prices.

Judge Question 1: “Can We Expect Prices to Go Down?”

Student Takeaway:
Judges reward realistic, economically sound answers — not optimism.
90+ Answer:
“Prices may stabilize, but large decreases usually require increased supply efficiency or reduced demand. Historically, prices adjust downward slowly.”

Judge Question 2: “How Can I Spend Less on Groceries?”

Student Takeaway:
Your advice must be practical and tied to economic reasoning.
90+ Answer:
“You can respond to price signals by buying store brands, substituting lower-cost goods, and timing purchases around sales. These choices reduce spending without lowering consumption.”
Student Rule:
Always explain why the advice works economically.

Final Scoring Truth

If you clearly explain:
  • Why prices changed
  • How markets respond
  • What the client should do
Judges have a clear reason to give you 15–18s.
