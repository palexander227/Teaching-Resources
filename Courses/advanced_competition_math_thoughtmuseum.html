Competition mathematics is often mistaken for speed. In its best form, however, it is not about rushing through tricks. It is about learning how to enter an unfamiliar problem, notice hidden structure, choose a promising path, and explain the solution with clarity.

This seminar is designed for mathematically ambitious students who are ready to move beyond routine exercises and toward creative problem solving. The course draws inspiration from AMC, AIME, olympiad-style problems, and classical problem-solving traditions, but the real goal is deeper: to develop mathematical taste, courage, strategy, and proof-writing discipline.

Students learn to ask better questions before they try to calculate faster.

Each week focuses on a different region of the problem-solving map: combinatorics, number theory, geometry, algebra, and proof strategy. The journey ends with a curated mini-olympiad and polished written solutions suitable for a student portfolio.

Five Weeks of Exploration

The weekly sequence is designed to feel like a guided expedition through the major territories of advanced high-school problem solving.

AMC · AIME · Olympiad
Week1

Combinatorial Thinking

Students learn how to count without losing control: cases, complements, bijections, Pascal-type reasoning, and the pigeonhole principle.

  • casework
  • inclusion-exclusion
  • pigeonhole
  • counting paths
  • symmetry
Challenge Studio

Design two different solutions to the same counting problem and compare the hidden structure each one reveals.

Week2

Number-Theoretic Methods

Students explore divisibility, congruences, residues, primes, Diophantine equations, and the art of reducing a problem modulo a carefully chosen number.

  • modular arithmetic
  • divisibility
  • prime factorization
  • remainders
  • invariants
Challenge Studio

Use modular reasoning to prove impossibility: not every numerical pattern can exist.

Week3

Geometric Problem Solving

Students study angle chasing, similarity, cyclic quadrilaterals, power of a point, and the practice of adding the right auxiliary line at the right moment.

  • similarity
  • angle chasing
  • cyclic figures
  • power of a point
  • auxiliary lines
Challenge Studio

Turn a complicated diagram into a small network of ratios, angles, and circles.

Week4

Algebraic Tactics

Students investigate polynomials, substitutions, inequalities, functional equations, and algebraic transformations that reveal concealed structure.

  • factoring
  • substitution
  • inequalities
  • functional equations
  • polynomials
Challenge Studio

Transform an ugly expression into a recognizable object by choosing the right substitution.

Week5

Proof, Strategy, and Mathematical Creativity

Students learn how to select problems, manage time, write solutions, revise arguments, and reflect on the creative process behind successful problem solving.

  • strategy
  • proof writing
  • solution polishing
  • reflection
  • mini-olympiad
Final Studio

Complete a curated mini-olympiad and submit polished solutions with strategy notes.

Competition Mathematics as Creative Training

The purpose of this seminar is not merely to prepare for a contest. It is to help students become more agile, courageous, and articulate mathematical thinkers. The student learns to enter difficult problems with curiosity, search for structure, and turn insight into proof.
