Every day, billions of messages move through public networks: texts, bank transactions, passwords, medical records, photographs, and private conversations. The astonishing fact is that these messages can travel through open channels and still remain secure.

This seminar introduces students to the mathematical ideas that make that possible. We begin with playful classical ciphers, move into modular arithmetic and codebreaking, and then discover how abstract algebra becomes a practical language for digital security.

Students do not merely learn about cryptography. They build working cryptographic tools, explain the mathematics behind them, and see how beautiful abstraction becomes real-world power.

Big ideas

Six mathematical ideas anchor the seminar.

Each idea is introduced through concrete puzzles, then developed into a more powerful mathematical or computational tool.

Math + code + proof
1

Secrecy as Structure

A cipher is not magic. It is a transformation with rules, weaknesses, and a mathematical shape.

2

Modular Worlds

Clock arithmetic turns ordinary numbers into finite systems where encryption can be designed and analyzed.

3

Inverses and Keys

To decrypt a message, we must reverse a transformation. The idea of inverse becomes central.

4

Groups and Symmetry

Groups organize repeated actions, permutations, rotations, and cryptographic transformations.

5

Algorithms as Mathematics

Python becomes a laboratory where students test conjectures and turn theorems into working code.

6

Trust Without Secrecy

Public-key cryptography shows how two people can communicate securely even when the method is public.

Five-week seminar journey

A complete course path from puzzles to RSA.

The weekly structure is rich enough for a gifted student, but accessible enough to be taught as an engaging summer enrichment seminar.

5 weeks · 10+ labs
Week01

The Art of Secret Writing

Students begin with classical cryptography: Caesar ciphers, substitution ciphers, affine ciphers, and the first experience of attacking a system by looking for patterns.

MathematicsFunctions, transformations, modular alphabets.
Python LabBuild Caesar and affine cipher tools.
ChallengeBreak a mystery message using frequency analysis.
Week02

The Clockwork Ocean of Modular Arithmetic

Students learn congruences, modular inverses, the Euclidean algorithm, and the arithmetic structure that makes cryptography possible.

MathematicsCongruence, gcd, inverses, units modulo n.
Python LabImplement gcd and modular inverse routines.
ChallengeFind when an affine cipher can be decrypted.
Week03

Symmetry, Permutations, and Groups

Students encounter abstract algebra as a language for structure: groups, closure, identity, inverses, Cayley tables, and permutation actions.

MathematicsGroups, permutations, cyclic groups, Cayley tables.
Python LabGenerate operation tables and permutation systems.
ChallengeDesign a cipher from permutations and analyze it.
Week04

Prime Numbers and Public-Key Cryptography

Students study primes, Euler's totient function, Fermat/Euler ideas, and the architecture of RSA encryption and decryption.

MathematicsPrimes, φ(n), Euler's theorem, modular exponentiation.
Python LabBuild a working toy RSA implementation.
ChallengeExplain why decryption reverses encryption.
Week05

Building a Secure Communication System

Students integrate the course by designing, coding, testing, and explaining a small cryptographic system with a final presentation.

MathematicsSecurity assumptions, signatures, verification.
Python LabPackage code into a readable final notebook.
ChallengePresent the system as a mathematical artifact.
Final exploration project

Build a working cryptographic artifact.

The capstone is designed to feel like a small research-and-build project. Students produce code, mathematical explanation, and a final presentation that shows both technical understanding and creative ownership.

RSA MessengerBuild an encoder/decoder for small integer messages using public and private keys.
Codebreaker ToolkitCreate Python tools for attacking classical ciphers and reporting evidence.
Group ExplorerVisualize a finite group or permutation system and connect it to encryption.
Digital Signature DemoShow how a message can be verified without revealing a private key.

Beyond the seminar

Where this course can lead.

Students who enjoy this seminar may continue toward number theory, abstract algebra, cybersecurity, computer science, algorithm design, finite fields, elliptic curve cryptography, blockchain protocols, or the mathematics of the fast Fourier transform.

More importantly, they experience a central ThoughtMuseum principle: abstraction is not escape from reality. At its best, abstraction is how reality becomes intelligible, programmable, and secure.
