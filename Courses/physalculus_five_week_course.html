Physalculus

A comprehensible guide for the inquisitive and curious.

Physics asks what the world does. Calculus asks how change itself can be measured. This course places them side by side—so motion, force, energy, slope, derivative, and accumulation begin to read like parts of one intelligible story.

Students often meet these subjects as separate collections of procedures. In Physalculus, velocity is not merely a formula—it is the slope of a position graph. Acceleration is the change in that slope. Work and impulse are not isolated rules—they are accumulated effects.

This is a preview with strategic review, not an attempt to compress two yearlong AP courses into five weeks. AP Physics 1 remains algebra-based; calculus enters here as an illuminating lens that reveals where the familiar equations come from and how their graphs fit together.

The shared language

One phenomenon. Four representations. No hiding behind a formula.

Both AP Physics and AP Calculus reward students who can move among pictures, graphs, numbers, equations, and words. Every major investigation in this course is viewed through all four lenses.

Graphical

Read slope, curvature, signed area, turning points, and the physical story encoded in a curve.

1.2, 1.9, 3.4…

Numerical

Use tables, measured data, finite differences, estimates, and units to infer what a system is doing.

F = ma

Symbolic

Build equations from physical laws, manipulate them carefully, and interpret every symbol.

“why?”

Verbal

Make a claim, support it with evidence, and explain what the mathematics means in the actual system.

Five-week journey

From “the graph goes up” to a defensible model of motion.

Each week begins with a curiosity question, supplies just enough mathematical review, develops the physics, and ends with a visible artifact. The course deliberately alternates between intuition and precision.

W1

The World as a Graph

How can a curve tell a physical story?

Students review functions, units, dimensional reasoning, slope, trigonometry, and vectors while learning to distinguish position, displacement, speed, velocity, and acceleration. Motion diagrams and x–t, v–t, and a–t graphs become translations of the same event.

W2

The Instant

How can motion have a rate at one exact moment?

Average velocity tightens into instantaneous velocity through numerical zooming, secant slopes, tangent lines, and limit intuition. Students connect the first derivative to velocity and the second derivative to acceleration, then apply the ideas to free fall and projectile motion.

W3

Forces Write the Curve

What invisible interaction produced the acceleration?

Newton’s laws, free-body diagrams, friction, tension, equilibrium, and circular motion are linked to the changing slope of velocity. Students learn to move in both directions: force → acceleration → motion, and observed motion → inferred net force.

W4

Accumulation, Work, and Energy

When does area become a physical quantity?

Rectangular sums become definite integrals; areas under velocity, force–time, and force–position graphs become displacement, impulse, and work. The work–energy theorem, gravitational potential energy, spring energy, power, and conservation are organized as an energy ledger.

W5

Momentum, Rotation, and the Hidden Force

Can the cause be recovered from the evidence?

Impulse and momentum provide a second language for interaction. Rotational motion and oscillation preview how familiar ideas reappear in new forms. Students then assemble the course into a capstone case: infer the governing physics from motion data and communicate the model across representations.

One model, three views

For constant acceleration, the familiar kinematics equations are not unrelated formulas. They are a derivative-and-accumulation family.

x(t) = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²
v(t) = dx/dt = v₀ + at
a(t) = dv/dt = a

The graph shapes reveal the mathematics: constant acceleration gives a horizontal a–t graph, a linear v–t graph, and a quadratic x–t graph.

AP habits of mind

The course trains the moves that make advanced work manageable.

01

Translate, do not merely calculate.

Move among verbal descriptions, diagrams, tables, graphs, and equations without losing the physical meaning.

02

Reason symbolically before substituting numbers.

Identify knowns, unknowns, signs, units, and governing principles before reaching for a calculator.

03

Justify the graph.

Explain why a graph rises, falls, bends, crosses zero, or encloses a physically meaningful area.

04

Interrogate the answer.

Check dimensions, limiting cases, direction, magnitude, and whether the result makes sense in the real system.

05

Write the final sentence.

An answer is not complete until the student states what the result means in context.

The unifying map
x(t) ⟶ v(t) ⟶ a(t) ⟶ F(t)
∫ vdt   ∫ Fdt   ∫ Fdx

Differentiation moves from a quantity to its rate of change. Integration recovers accumulated change. Physics tells us which relationship matters and why.

The result is not “calculus pasted onto physics.” It is a coherent map of motion, cause, and accumulation.

Final investigation

The Hidden Force Case File

A mysterious system leaves evidence: a video, a position table, a noisy graph, or a force trace. The student must determine what happened, identify the governing relationships, test a model, and defend the conclusion.

One motion becomes an argument.

The finished portfolio is compact but serious: it must connect observation to law and mathematical representation without pretending that a graph or equation explains itself.

Selected course spine

The texts are used as laboratories of ideas—not as page-count obligations.

Selections are drawn from the uploaded physics and calculus texts. Reading is targeted, and the course emphasizes conceptual examples, graphs, reasoning strategies, and carefully chosen problems.

Physics spine

Cutnell & Johnson, Physics, 9th ed.
  • Mathematical concepts, units, trigonometry, scalars, vectors, and components
  • One- and two-dimensional kinematics, free fall, and projectile motion
  • Forces, Newton’s laws, equilibrium, friction, tension, and circular motion
  • Work, energy, power, impulse, momentum, rotation, and oscillation previews

Calculus spine

Finney et al., Calculus: Graphical, Numerical, Algebraic, 5th ed.
  • Functions, graphs, increments, slope, domain, and multiple representations
  • Average and instantaneous rates of change, limits, and tangent lines
  • Derivatives, velocity, acceleration, local linearity, and sensitivity
  • Finite sums, definite integrals, accumulation, and the Fundamental Theorem

Course scope is intentionally selective. It previews central AP Physics 1 and AP Calculus AB ideas while preserving time for experiment, interpretation, and genuine understanding.

What the student gains

A head start measured in understanding—not in rushed chapter coverage.

Approach AP-style prompts with a repeatable reasoning process.
Complete a polished, portfolio-ready investigation.

Readiness and fit

Best for inquisitive students entering or considering AP Physics 1, AP Calculus AB, advanced precalculus, or an engineering pathway.

Strong Algebra 2 skills are helpful. Familiarity with functions, graphs, basic trigonometry, and scientific notation is useful—but the most important prerequisite is a willingness to ask why.

No prior calculus course is required. The mathematical depth can be adjusted upward for students who already know derivative rules or downward for students who need a more visual introduction.

This is a preview, bridge, and conceptual launchpad. It is not presented as a substitute for either full AP course.
