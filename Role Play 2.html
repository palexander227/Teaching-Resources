How to Excel in the DECA Apparel & Accessories Marketing Role Play

This guide is written for students preparing for the Apparel and Accessories Marketing Series Event. Its purpose is to help you move beyond “knowing the content” and toward earning top evaluation scores.

Big Idea: Judges are not looking for creativity alone. They are scoring how effectively you apply marketing concepts to a real business situation under time pressure.

1. Understand the Role You Are Playing

In this event, you are not a student presenting a school project. You are acting as a promotional director speaking to a global brand president.

  • Your language should be professional.
  • Your tone should be confident but respectful.
  • Your ideas should sound implementable, not theoretical.
Think like this:
“If I were actually hired for this role, would this plan sound realistic to an executive?”

2. Master the Performance Indicators (This Is Non-Negotiable)

Your entire score is driven by how well you demonstrate the required Performance Indicators (PIs). Top competitors intentionally design their presentation to hit each one clearly.

PI 1: Identify Components of a Retail Image

  • Store atmosphere (layout, lighting, cleanliness)
  • Brand personality and values
  • Customer experience
  • Consistency across locations
High-level move:
Connect the unusual services (study space, laundromat) directly to the brand image you want college students to associate with RE-DRESSED.

PI 2: Explain the Nature of Effective Communications

  • Clear message
  • Consistent tone
  • Audience-appropriate language
  • Two-way communication

PI 3: Identify the Elements of the Promotional Mix

  • Advertising
  • Sales promotions
  • Public relations
  • Personal selling
  • Digital and social media

PI 4: Describe Word-of-Mouth Channels

  • Social sharing
  • Campus influencers
  • Peer recommendations
  • User-generated content

PI 5: Explain Types of Advertising Media

  • Digital ads
  • Social platforms
  • Out-of-home advertising
  • In-store promotions
Strategy Tip: You do not need to list everything. You need to explain a few choices clearly and justify why they fit the target market.

3. Structure a Winning 10-Minute Presentation

Recommended Flow

  1. Opening (1 minute): Purpose and big idea
  2. Brand & Retail Image (2 minutes)
  3. Promotional Mix Strategy (4 minutes)
  4. Word-of-Mouth & Advertising Media (2 minutes)
  5. Wrap-Up (1 minute): Success metrics
Strong opening line:
“RE-DRESSED is not just a store — it’s a lifestyle space designed for modern college students.”

4. Prepare for the Judge’s Questions (This Is Where Scores Swing)

Judges are instructed to ask you specific questions. Top competitors anticipate these and answer confidently.

Question 1: Employee Training

  • Explain how service expectations differ
  • Highlight customer engagement skills
  • Show consistency with the DRESSED brand

Question 2: Product Choice

  • Acknowledge why denim makes sense
  • Suggest data-driven alternatives
  • Explain flexibility in future merchandising

Question 3: Measuring Success

  • Foot traffic
  • Dwell time
  • Repeat visits
  • Social engagement
  • Sales per square foot
Judge Mindset: They are listening for business logic, not guesses.

5. What Separates “Meets” from “Exceeds Expectations”

  • Clear structure
  • Professional language
  • Explicit PI connections
  • Confident responses to questions
  • Creative ideas grounded in reality
Avoid This:
  • Rambling
  • Listing buzzwords without explanation
  • Ignoring the target market
  • Failing to justify decisions

6. Final Advice

You are being evaluated as a future business professional. If your plan sounds thoughtful, structured, and actionable, judges will reward it.

Prepare like a professional. Present like a consultant. Think like an executive.
